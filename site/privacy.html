Relay privacy policy

Last updated: 17 October 2026

Relay is made by Vythera. This policy explains what Relay does with your information. The short version: everything Relay handles goes directly between your own devices, and we never receive any of it.

What Relay does not collect

  • Your content. Files, photos, text, links, clipboard contents, notifications, contact search results and everything else Relay moves are sent straight from one of your devices to another over your local network, encrypted between the two devices. They never reach us, and there is no Relay server they could pass through.
  • Accounts. Relay has no sign-in, so we hold no name, email address or profile.
  • Your contact card. The card you can share by holding two phones together is the one you type into Relay yourself, and it is sent only to the device you touch.

Device tools

After you pair two devices, each can use some tools on the other. Three are available as soon as you pair: status (battery level, whether it is charging, the type of network and the media volume), finding the device (making it ring) and ping. Every other tool is off until you turn it on, separately for each paired device, on the device that would be accessed. You can turn any of them off again at any time, and forgetting a device removes all of its permissions.

On an Android phone, you can allow a paired device to:

  • See your notifications. The paired device can list the notifications on your phone, show new ones as they arrive, reply where the app offers a reply, and dismiss them. This needs Android's notification access for Relay as well as your permission for that device. You can hide the notifications of chosen apps from every paired device. Notifications from Relay itself, and those Android marks as secret, are never sent.
  • Search your contacts. The paired device can search your contacts by name and receive the matching names and phone numbers, at most 50 at a time. Relay never sends your whole address book, and does not change your contacts.
  • Check and silence calls. The paired device can see whether the phone is idle, ringing or in a call, and silence a ringing call. Relay does not read your call history, and does not answer, end or record calls.
  • Browse one folder. The paired device can list and download files only from the single folder you choose. Nothing outside it is reachable.
  • Control media and volume. Play, pause, skip and change the volume, and see what is playing.

On a computer, you can allow a paired device to:

  • Move the mouse, click, scroll and type; control presentations; lock the screen; keep the computer awake for up to 30 minutes; run commands that you yourself saved in Relay on that computer; control media and volume; and browse the one folder you choose.

Other things Relay uses

  • Camera (optional). To scan the QR code of another device when connecting. The camera image is read on your phone to find the code; nothing is saved or sent.
  • Your installed apps (optional). When you choose to send an app, Relay lists the apps on your phone so you can pick one, then sends that app's installation files to the device you chose. Your app data and sign-ins are not included.
  • Update checks (computer apps; can be turned off). Shortly after starting, and then once a day, Relay for Windows, macOS and Linux asks GitHub, where it is published, for the number of the latest version. The request carries nothing about you or your devices, but like any web request it reaches GitHub from your network address; see GitHub's privacy statement. Turn it off in Settings › About. The Android app is updated by Google Play.

What stays on your device

  • The devices you have paired with, their names and their public keys.
  • The permissions you have given each paired device.
  • A history of what you sent and received, so the app can show it to you.
  • Your settings, and the files you received, in the folder you chose.

Removing the app removes all of it.

Optional usage statistics

Relay can send anonymous usage statistics through Google Firebase Analytics. This is off unless you turn it on in Settings › Privacy.

When it is on, Relay reports that something happened — a device was paired, a transfer finished or failed, the clipboard was shared — along with a coarse category such as "photos". It never includes file names, file contents, text, clipboard contents, notifications, contacts, device names, network addresses, or anything identifying the devices you pair with. Firebase also collects an app-instance identifier and general technical information such as the app version and country; see Google's Firebase privacy page.

You can turn it off again at any time in the same place, which stops collection.

Permissions Relay asks for

  • Local network / Wi-Fi state. To find and reach your other devices.
  • Notifications. To tell you what arrived and to show transfers in progress.
  • Run in the background. So your devices can reach this one when the app is not open.
  • Notification access (optional). Only for letting a paired device see your notifications.
  • Contacts (optional). Only for letting a paired device search your contacts.
  • Phone state and call control (optional). Only for letting a paired device check and silence calls.
  • Camera (optional). Only for scanning QR codes.
  • Change audio settings. So a paired device can change the media volume.
  • NFC (optional). To share your contact card by holding two phones together.

Relay does not ask for access to your SMS messages or your call history.

Children

Relay is not directed at children.
